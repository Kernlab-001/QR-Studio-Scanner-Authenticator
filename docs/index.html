Privacy Policy — QR Studio

Last updated: 26 October 2026 · Applies to QR Studio — Scanner & Authenticator (Chrome extension), version 2.4.1

QR Studio never transmits, sells or shares your QR data, and collects nothing in the background. Everything you scan, create or store stays on your device, inside your own Chrome profile.

The only thing the extension ever sends anywhere is a sign-in request to the account server, and only if you choose to create an account. There is no telemetry, no analytics and no crash reporting.

The data you deliberately save — your authenticator secrets and your opt-in scan history — is stored locally only. It is never sent to us or to anyone else, because there is no server to send it to.

Data handling disclosure

Chrome Web Store requires every extension that handles user data to disclose it, even when the data is stored only on the user's own device. QR Studio handles exactly four categories:

Data categoryWhat it isPurposeWhere it goes
Authentication information The TOTP/HOTP secret, issuer, account name, algorithm, digits, period and counter of each authenticator you save in the vault App functionality — generating your rotating codes chrome.storage.local on your device. Never transmitted.
User activity The QR payloads you scan, when you turn on “Save general QR scans on this device” (off by default) App functionality — showing your own scan history chrome.storage.local on your device. Never transmitted.
Purchase information If you buy a Pro licence: the licence key you paste in, and the email address inside it App functionality — proving that the paid features are unlocked chrome.storage.local on your device. Never transmitted.
Personal information The email address you sign in with, if you create an account App functionality — signing in, and remembering who you are To the account server (Supabase) for authentication, and chrome.storage.local on your device. Nothing else is sent with it.

No other data category is handled. Nothing is collected automatically, nothing is sent anywhere, and no developer or third party can read it.

Limited Use disclosure

The use of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. QR Studio does not use any Google API. It does not transfer user data to third parties, does not use user data for advertising, and does not allow any human being to read the data it stores.

What the extension does not do

  • It does not send QR code contents to any server.
  • It does not include analytics, telemetry, crash reporting, A/B testing or advertising.
  • It does not use a remote configuration or update-ping service.
  • It does not load any remote scripts, fonts, stylesheets or images.
  • It does not read, modify or transmit the content of web pages.
  • It does not require an account, and there is nothing to sign up for.
  • It does not use advertising or tracking identifiers.
  • The only request it ever makes is a sign-in request to the account server, and only when you choose to sign in.

Accounts

The account is optional. Everything in QR Studio works without one — scanning, the authenticator vault, the history and QR creation are free and need no sign-in.

If you create an account:

  • Your email address and password go to the account server (Supabase), which handles the sign-in. Your password is never stored by QR Studio and never passes through the extension.
  • The extension keeps the signed-in email address and a session token in chrome.storage.local on your device, so it can tell whether you are signed in.
  • Nothing else is ever sent. No QR content, no vault secrets, no scan history, no usage data. The extension never asks the account server anything about how you use it.
  • Signing out removes the session from the device.

Pro features need a licence key. The key is checked offline, so it works whether or not you are signed in — the account only decides where you are sent when you click a Pro feature.

The only network permission in the extension's manifest is activeTab, which Chrome grants on demand. It is used in exactly two ways, both triggered by an explicit click you make:

  1. capturing the visible tab as an image when you choose the Tab scan source, so the QR code on screen can be decoded;
  2. opening a result link or the full-screen camera page in a new tab.

Paid features

QR Studio has a paid tier, QR Studio Pro. The store listing says so plainly, and this policy explains what it changes.

What stays free, for everyone, with no account and no limit: scanning from the camera, an image, the current tab or pasted content; the authenticator vault with TOTP and HOTP; encrypted backup and restore; the local scan history; and creating a single QR code with the full styling options.

What Pro adds: bulk import from other authenticator apps, and batch QR creation. Both run entirely on your device.

How the licence is checked, and what that means for your privacy

  • A licence key is signed by the project key and verified inside the extension with the public key only. The check happens locally, so activating Pro makes no network request and the extension still never contacts a server.
  • There is no account, no sign-in, no device registration and no telemetry. The extension cannot tell whether you have a licence beyond what is on your own screen.
  • The licence key, and the email address inside it, are stored in chrome.storage.local on your device and are never transmitted.
  • Payment itself happens on the payment provider’s own website, which is opened by you. The extension is not involved in the payment and never sees your card details.

Links to other pages

The extension contains one link that leaves the extension: a Support development link in the History tab and in the footer of the full-page view. Clicking it opens this project’s support page in a new browser tab, exactly as clicking a scanned link does.

  • The extension loads nothing from that page. It is a plain link, so the extension itself still makes no network request of any kind.
  • Nothing about you is passed along. The page carries no identifier, no referrer data about your vault and no scan history.
  • The support page is a static file served by GitHub Pages, and any payment is handled by the payment provider you choose on that page (for example GitHub Sponsors). That provider may set its own cookies and has its own privacy policy. We never receive your payment details, and no part of your vault is ever involved.

What is stored locally

DataStorage locationWhen it is storedWhen it is deleted
Vault accounts (issuer, account name, OTP secret, algorithm, digits, period, counter, favourite flag) chrome.storage.local Only when you explicitly save an account When you delete the account, or uninstall the extension
Generated OTP codes Computed in memory only Never written to disk Immediately when the vault reloads or the popup closes
General scan history chrome.storage.local Only if you enable “Save general QR scans on this device” — off by default When you clear the history, or uninstall the extension
User settings (scan intent, source preference, history toggle) chrome.storage.local When you change them When you uninstall the extension
Encrypted vault backups A file you choose on your disk Only when you click Backup Only by you — the extension never writes backup files on its own

Authenticator setup secrets are never written to scan history. A QR code identified as an authenticator setup code is redacted from the result, is never copied to the clipboard automatically, and must be explicitly saved by you.

How the data is protected

  • Vault secrets are stored in chrome.storage.local, inside your Chrome profile. This storage is not encrypted by the extension — it inherits the protection of your operating system account and your Chrome profile encryption. Anyone with access to your unlocked profile can read it.
  • When you export a backup, the file is encrypted locally before it is written to disk: key derivation is PBKDF2, SHA-256, 210,000 iterations, with a random 16-byte salt; encryption is AES-GCM with a 256-bit key and a random 12-byte IV; the format identifier is qr-studio-encrypted-v1.
  • QR Studio never stores, logs or transmits your backup password. If you lose it, the backup cannot be recovered.
  • Restoring a backup decrypts it locally in the extension; the plaintext never leaves your device.

Third-party code

The extension bundles three open-source libraries that run entirely inside the browser: jsQR (Apache-2.0) and zxing-wasm (MIT, a WebAssembly build of the Apache-2.0 ZXing engine) for offline QR decoding, and QR Code generator for JavaScript (MIT) for local QR creation. None of them performs network requests: the WebAssembly module is read from the extension package, and the address the upstream build would fetch it from is overridden. See THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md.

Children

QR Studio does not knowingly collect any personal information from anyone, including children under 13, because it does not collect any information at all.
